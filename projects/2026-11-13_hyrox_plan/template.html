How it follows EPI programming

  • EPI training system: consultation → needs analysis → screening → testing → profile → periodization → program design. Your OHS screen (4/12, average) and ROM sheet from the NCSC course decide the correctives in Phase 1: hip extension 25° (target 30–45°), hip internal rotation 15° (target 30–45°), SLR 70° (target 90°), left ER 40° (target 60°).
  • Needs analysis (HYROX): 8 × 1 km of running between 8 stations. Primary: aerobic power and threshold (MAS, MLSS), compromised running, lactate capacity and strength endurance on the stations. Secondary: max strength and strength-speed (sleds, lunges, carries), plus muscle retention. Injury focus: calves and Achilles, hamstrings and lower back under fatigue.
  • Model: conjugate (EPI Phase 2 for trained athletes). Several qualities are trained in parallel, with one emphasis per block and wave-like loading: 3 weeks of overload, then a de-load. Your phases are kept, and week 4 becomes the EPI de-load and retest week. Weeks 7–8 replace the next de-load with a sharpen-and-taper.
  • Themes: EXTENSIVE volume (aerobic, hypertrophy, correctives) → INTENSIVE intensity (threshold and VO₂max intervals, max strength, strength-speed, specific work) → sharpen → taper.

Testing and training zones

1.5 km time trial (week 1 Mon · retest week 4 Thu)

  • 15 min warm-up: easy jog, drills, 3 × 80 m building strides.
  • Run 1.5 km flat-out on a track or flat route. Record the time in seconds.
  • MAS (m/s) = 1500 ÷ time. Example: 5:30 = 330 s → 4.55 m/s (16.4 km/h).
  • Pace for a % of MAS: seconds per km = 1000 ÷ (MAS × %).

Strength tests (week 1)

  • Work up to a clean 5RM in trap-bar deadlift, back squat, bench press and pull-up (add weight if you can do more than 10).
  • Estimated 1RM ≈ 5RM × 1.13. All % loads use this number.
  • If a lift moves slower than planned on the day, use the RIR target and drop the load. Don't chase the %.

Week by week

Every hard run starts with 15 min easy plus running drills (A-march, A-skip, B-skip, high knees, 3 × 80 m strides). Technique cues: tall posture, light quick steps (around 170–180 per minute), foot landing under the hips, relaxed arms.

Keep your muscle (مش عايز أخسر عضلات)

The rules that protect muscle during a running block

  • Keep lifting heavy, cut the volume. Strength and muscle are kept with much less volume as long as intensity stays high. That's why the main lifts stay at 75–85 % right up to week 8, while the sets come down.
  • 2 gym sessions a week until week 6, one in week 7, a short primer in race week. Upper-body hypertrophy assistance (rows, presses, laterals, arms) stays in every phase, because running doesn't train it.
  • Manage the interference effect: put hard lower-body gym and hard running on the same day (run first, gym 6 h or more later), or put gym after an easy run. Keep easy days easy.
  • Protein: 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight a day, spread over 4–5 meals of about 0.4 g/kg each (including one before bed).
  • Don't diet during the prep. Eat at maintenance, or a very small deficit at most. A big deficit plus high running volume is the main way to lose muscle. Put most of your carbs around the key sessions.
  • Sleep 7–9 h. Watch your weight trend weekly. If your weight keeps falling and gym performance drops, add food before you cut training.

Evidence vs coaching judgment: the protein range, keeping intensity to maintain strength, and spacing strength and endurance work are research-based. The exact session order, the station circuits and the simulation dates are coaching judgment for this calendar, and should be adjusted to how you respond.

Race week and race day (Friday 8 January)

Race week

  • Volume −50–60 %, keep short bits of race pace (Tue openers), and nothing new: same shoes, kit and food.
  • Wed–Thu: carb-focused meals (rice, pasta, bread, fruit), normal protein, less fibre and fat on Thursday evening. Drink to thirst plus electrolytes.
  • Check your wave time, division weights and Roxzone layout. Plan breakfast 3 h before your start.

Warm-up (25–30 min before your wave)

  • Raise: 6–8 min easy jog. Activate: glute bridges, band walks, calf raises. Mobilize: leg swings, hip openers, 90/90. Potentiate: 3 × 15 s strides, 5 wall balls, 10 s SkiErg pulls.

Pacing

  • Run 1: controlled, about 5–10 s per km slower than your target race pace. Most of the time lost in a HYROX goes in the second half.
  • Runs 2–7: steady at race pace. Use the first 200 m after a station to settle your breathing before you speed up.
  • Sleds: low body angle, short fast steps, don't stop mid-lane. Wall balls: break early in planned sets (e.g. 15-15-10…) rather than going to failure.
  • Run 8: everything left.

Monitoring and adjusting

  • Each morning, check four signals: sleep, resting heart rate (vs your normal), soreness and motivation. If 2 or more are clearly off, swap that day's hard session for an easy one and move on. Don't make it up later.
  • Log RPE (1–10) after each session. If an "RPE 7" session feels like a 9 two weeks running, cut that week's volume by 20–30 %.
  • Pain rule: pain above 3/10, or pain that changes how you run, stops running that day. Calf or Achilles pain gets attention immediately.
  • Missed a session? Skip it. Never double up. In weeks 7–8, less is always the safer choice.
