# Gym hype edits (2026-10-15)

Three folders of raw iPhone training footage (24 + 32 + 17 clips, HEVC 10-bit HLG / Dolby Vision, 1080x1920 and
2160x3840, one 120 fps clip) -> three 37.6 s Reels (35.2 s of edit + the OSM end card), one per folder.

| Video | Folder | Look | Music (original, tools/music.py "phonk") | Titles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OSM_GYM_01_dont-quit | 1EGsOpci… (arm day, light-blue top) | steel | F minor, 150 bpm, seed 5 | ARM DAY, NO DAYS OFF |
| OSM_GYM_02_built-not-born | 1VRMhL1b… (black tank, daylight) | grit | D minor, 150 bpm, seed 9 | BUILT NOT BORN, NO SHORTCUTS |
| OSM_GYM_03_no-limits | 1XcioCB-… (black tee, dark neon) | neon | G minor, 150 bpm, seed 13 | NO LIMITS, DISCIPLINE |

Structure (same for all three, every cut on a beat: 150 bpm = 12 frames per beat):
intro 8 beats (hook + drop flash at frame 0) -> build 8 (cuts accelerate, freeze on the stop) -> drop 1 at 6.4 s
(32 beats: 4-beat shots, speed ramps 1x -> 0.35x -> 1.8x on the best reps, beat punch-ins) -> break 8 (slow motion
+ title; folder 1 uses the real 120 fps capture) -> build 8 -> drop 2 at 25.6 s (24 beats) -> end card.

Tools added: `tools/footage_review.py` (contact sheets + motion/audio curves per clip), `projects/_gym/montage.py`
(beat-grid montage builder: auto-picks the most explosive window of each clip, ramps, holds, drops), music style
`phonk` (half-time snare, clipped 808 slides, cowbell riff), studio `pulses` (beat punch-ins) and `flashes`.
Fixed: `render_edit.dims()` read the Dolby Vision record instead of the rotation (portrait clips were cropped 2x).

Decisions:
- Clip audio muted: the gym's own speakers play copyrighted music in most clips; the soundtrack is the original
  music + the in-house SFX kit (impacts, booms, whips, risers), both made here, so safe for social media.
- Folder 1: the exact BT.2020 -> 709 conversion turned the light-blue shirt cyan; the plain HLG decode looked natural.
- Folder 3: `9E93C5B9.mp4` is an earlier export with burned-in "Me to Me" text; the same take `IMG_8147.MOV` is used.
- No motion interpolation (no warped bodies): slow motion is frame-blended, or true 120 fps.
