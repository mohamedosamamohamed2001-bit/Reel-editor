### Lecture 1: Course introduction (p.10-23)
- Outcomes: course content; EPI coaching philosophy & methodology; EPI coaching framework & training system (p.10).
- Phase 2 content (p.11): D1 strength & power + advanced program design, practical snatch + upper-body strength & power;
  D2 max-velocity speed & ESD, practical max velocity + lower-body strength & power; D3 conjugate periodization + athlete case study,
  practical fast SSC plyometrics + ESD testing & training.
- NCSC exam (p.11): written MCQ 60 min on Phase 1 & 2 manuals, pass 70 %; practical Olympic weightlifting 30 min (demonstration
  skills, technical and coaching models), pass 70 %.
- History (p.12): 2012-14 Ireland/N. Ireland; 2015-17 Europe (Poland, UK, Germany, Czech Rep., Slovakia); 2018-25 global (China, UAE,
  Romania, Egypt, Saudi Arabia). Teams: Leinster, Ulster, IRFU, World Rugby, RWC 2023, Man Utd, FACR, Olympics, Legia, Egypt.
- S&C coach = two key areas (p.13-14): GYM-based coaching skills: strength, power, hypertrophy training, prehabilitation.
  PITCH-based: warm-ups, linear speed, agility, ESD.
- FOUR PILLARS of effective S&C coaching (p.14): 1 Philosophy (clear S&C philosophy), 2 Methodology (how to coach new movement
  skills), 3 Framework (step-by-step process for enhancing athleticism), 4 System (roadmap for your S&C coaching).
- Pillar 1 philosophy = set of beliefs/guiding principles that determine how you coach (p.15). Principles & beliefs: TECHNICAL
  ABILITY first (does the athlete have the mobility & stability for the movement? the coordination?) -> then PHYSICAL ABILITY
  (does the athlete have the required strength & power qualities?).
- Pillar 2 methodology (p.15-17): two tools:
  - TECHNICAL MODEL (T-model) = series of key points defining perfect form. Back squat T-model: feet shoulder/hip width & turned out;
    knees track in line with feet; torso stays upright; back remains flat; hips move towards ankles after 90 deg.
  - COACHING MODEL (C-model) = breaking a complex skill into a series of simpler movements. Back squat C-model: air squat ->
    goblet squat -> box squat -> back squat.
  - T&C models exist for GYM: squat pattern (back, front, overhead squat); hinge (RDL, deadlift); Olympic lifting (clean, jerk, snatch);
    plyometrics (slow SSC, fast SSC). PITCH: linear speed (acceleration, max velocity); agility (deceleration, turning, evasion/side-step).
- Pillar 3 coaching FRAMEWORK (p.17-19) = breakdown of components to develop athleticism, 5 steps:
  1 Athletic objectives -> 2 Physical quality(s) -> 3 Training methods -> 4 Exercise selection -> 5 Program variables.
  - Physical qualities (7): strength, power, speed, agility, endurance, mobility, hypertrophy.
  - Training method = a specific way of training that targets one physical quality (e.g. powerlifting = sport AND a method for max strength).
  - Program variables: sets & reps, intensity, volume (= training dosage), rest; manage them to elicit the desired adaptation.
- Pillar 4 EPI TRAINING SYSTEM (p.19), 8 steps: 1 athlete/head-coach consultation; 2 needs analysis; 3 movement screening;
  4 fitness testing; 5 profile results; 6 periodization plan & program design; 7 develop FV curve (strength-power);
  8 develop VI curve (velocity-intensity? "ESD-Speed").
- Worked example: football team (p.20-21): consultation (avg age 22, semi-pro, training age 2 y, 3rd division, hard-working, off-season,
  goals speed/power/fitness, previous hamstring strains + ankle sprains, 3 pitch + 1 gym per week, 90 min pitch / 60 min gym).
  Needs analysis: PRIMARY linear speed (acceleration), agility, speed endurance (ATP-CP fitness); SECONDARY power (reactive strength),
  aerobic fitness; INJURY profile: muscle strains 41 % of all injuries; hamstring strains 16 % of all injuries & 39.5 % of strains
  (Jones et al. 2021). Considerations: semi-pro, training age 2, movement skill unknown, 1 gym + 3 football sessions/wk, pre-season 6 weeks.
  Movement screening = MCS: overhead squat test + ROM testing. Fitness testing order: 1 bodyweight, 2 body composition, 3 leg power,
  4 speed, 5 strength, 6 energy system test. Profile (traffic lights): CMJ power poor <35 cm, average 35-40, good >40 cm;
  aerobic fitness Bronco poor >4:45 min, average 4:30-4:45, good <4:30 min. (p.21)
- Periodization plans (p.22): LINEAR = Hypertrophy -> Max strength -> Power (gym), linked with Aerobic fitness -> ATP-CP fitness ->
  Speed & agility (pitch). CONJUGATE = Strength endurance & hypertrophy -> Max strength & slow-SSC power -> Speed-strength & fast-SSC
  power, linked with Aerobic & anaerobic fitness -> ATP-CP fitness & acceleration speed -> Max-velocity speed & agility.
- Develop FV (force-velocity) curve (p.22): EXTENSIVE work = movement skill training, strength endurance, hypertrophy, prehabilitation.
  INTENSIVE work along the curve (high force/low velocity -> low force/high velocity): max strength -> explosive strength -> reactive strength.
- Develop VI (volume-intensity) curve (p.23): EXTENSIVE = aerobic system, anaerobic glycolysis system. INTENSIVE (high volume/low intensity
  -> low volume/high intensity on the curve): ATP-CP system -> agility training -> acceleration speed -> max velocity speed.
  (So step 8 "VI curve" = volume-intensity curve for ESD & speed.) Motto: "Keep it simple".

### Section 1: Fitness testing (p.24-36)
- Purpose: objective monitoring of fitness qualities over time with EPI's standardized battery (p.24).
- Testing session plan, consider (p.25): 1 time of season; 2 facilities (gym vs pitch); 3 equipment; 4 qualities to assess; 5 order of tests;
  6 group management (squad size). Battery wheel: body fat (4 sites), leg power (CMJ), speed (0-10 m & 0-30 m), strength (max strength vs
  strength endurance), endurance (aerobic vs anaerobic).
- Fitness testing summary table (p.26):
  | Component | Tests | Equipment |
  | Anthropometrics & body composition | height, weight, skinfold | stadiometer, digital scales, Harpenden calipers |
  | Leg power | CMJ; standing long jump (SLJ) [handwritten "Broad" next to SLJ] | electronic jump mat; long jump mat |
  | Speed | 0-10 m acceleration; 0-30 m max velocity | electronic timing gates (stopwatch alternative) |
  | Strength | bench press; back squat; chin up | bench; squat rack; chin bar |
  | Energy systems | Bronco (aerobic); 150 m shuttle (anaerobic glycolysis); phosphate decrement test (ATP-CP) | cones+stopwatch; cones+stopwatch; timing gates |
- Skinfolds (p.27-29): Harpenden calipers. Dry skin, before exercise. Pinch skin + subcutaneous fat with thumb/index; caliper prongs
  perpendicular 1-2 cm from fingers; release grip; read 1-2 s later to nearest 0.5 mm; one measurement per site then repeat all sites;
  if two readings differ <=10 % average them, otherwise more readings until two within 10 %.
  Sites: BICEPS vertical fold midway acromion-cubital fossa (front); TRICEPS vertical fold midway acromion-olecranon (back);
  SUBSCAPULAR diagonal fold 2 cm below inferior angle of scapula; SUPRAILIAC diagonal fold 1 cm above iliac crest.
  Table 1 (p.29) converts SUM of 4 folds to body fat %, separate male/female columns (e.g. sum 50 mm = 19.21 % M / 26.51 % F;
  100 mm = 27.80 % / 36.06 %; 30 mm = 13.07 / 19.71; 150 mm = 32.97 / 41.82; 200 mm = 36.71 / 46.00). Use the table for exact values.
- CMJ (p.30): Skyhook jump mat; feet hip/shoulder width; hands on hips throughout; squat down and immediately jump max; legs straight in
  the air; land on balls of feet then heels; reset feet; 3 jumps, best counts; measured in cm.
- Speed 0-10 m & 0-30 m (p.31): timing gates at start, 10 m and 30 m; athlete starts 0.7 m behind the start gate between two cones;
  TWO-POINT start; >=1 min between sprints; 3 sprints, fastest counts; seconds.
- IMTP isometric mid-thigh pull (p.32): MAT IMTP platform + Muscle Meter dynamometer via Bluetooth (Measurz app); stand on platform holding
  bar with straight arms, slight flexion at ankle/knee/hip; remove chain slack; on cue pull max for 3 s; isometric strength in kg.
- Estimated 1RM via VBT (p.33): Vitruve linear encoder; back squat; progressive loads: 3 reps at ~40 % est. 1RM, then 60 %, then 80 %;
  bar speed m/s; table (Medina 2010) load vs mean velocity, T1 column: 30 % 1.33; 35 % 1.21; 40 % 1.15; 45 % 1.06; 50 % 0.97; 55 % 0.89;
  60 % 0.80; 65 % 0.72; 70 % 0.64; 75 % 0.56; 80 % 0.48; 85 % 0.41; 90 % 0.33; 95 % 0.26; 100 % 0.19 m/s (+/- ~0.04-0.08);
  T1-T2 difference ~0.01 (reliable).
- 1RM back squat (p.34): closed pronated grip; bar above posterior deltoids at base of neck, grip slightly wider than shoulders; extend hips &
  knees to unrack; 1-2 steps back; feet shoulder width or wider, toes slightly out; controlled descent to 90 deg or deeper; 3 attempts; kg.
- 1RM bench press (p.35): supine, feet on floor, hips on bench; pronated grip slightly wider than shoulders; spotter at head end;
  lift off together, athlete says "my bar"; controlled lowering, touch chest, press to full elbow extension; 3 attempts; kg.
  (numbering in the manual skips 4-5.)
- 1RM chin up (p.36): weighted dip belt (e.g. +10 kg); closed SUPINATED grip; start from full hang; chin over bar and back to hang.
  NO REP if: chin not over bar; arms not fully extended at bottom; kipping with legs; knees rise above hips. 3 attempts; kg (added load).

### Section 2: Movement screening (p.37-47)
- MCS = Movement Compensation Screen: concise tool to identify problems in the musculoskeletal system and flag individuals possibly at risk
  of NON-CONTACT injury (p.37).
- Overhead squat set-up (p.38): feet hip/shoulder width and pointing STRAIGHT (if they rotate during a squat, reset before next rep);
  dowel grip wide enough to make a RIGHT ANGLE between upper and lower arm (athletes widen grip to hide mobility issues; coach must enforce).
- MCS scoresheet, MCS 1 Overhead Squat (OHS) test (p.39), 10 checkpoints:
  FRONT: 1 feet turn out (L/R); 2 knees buckle in (L/R). SIDE (yes/no): 3 heels off floor; 4 tibial angle <15 deg; 5 knee-dominant squat;
  6 lumbar spine rounds; 7 excessive forward lean; 8 arms fall forward; 9 squat depth <90 deg. REAR: 10 hip weight shift (L/R).
  Test score bands: GOOD 0-2, AVERAGE 3-5, POOR 6-12. (His own sheet in the PDF: name Osama, feet turn out R=1, tibial angle 1, lumbar
  rounds 1, weight shift R=1 -> OHS score 4 = average.)
- Identifying compensations (p.40-41) with photos: front view feet straight vs turned out; knees aligned vs buckle in. Side view list
  (heels off floor; tibial angle <15 deg; knee dominant; lumbar rounds; excessive forward lean; arms falling forward; depth <90 deg);
  photos heels on/off floor; tibial angle 15 deg (OK) vs <15 deg.
- More side/rear photos (p.42-44): neutral vs knee-dominant squat; neutral spine vs lumbar rounding; neutral torso vs excessive forward
  lean; arms aligned overhead vs falling forward; depth 90 deg vs <90 deg; REAR: neutral hips vs weight shift (to one side).
- ROM scoresheet (p.45-46) [his own measurements written in]: normative ROM targets:
  | Test | Muscles | Target ROM |
  | Straight leg raise (SLR) | biceps femoris, semimembranosus, semitendinosus (hamstrings) | 90 deg |
  | Knee to chest | hip mobility | 110-120 deg |
  | Prone hip extension | psoas, iliacus, rectus femoris, TFL | 30-45 deg |
  | Prone internal rotation | piriformis | 30-45 deg |
  | Prone external rotation | adductors, glute medius, glute minimus | 60 deg |
  | Prone knee flexion (passive) | rectus femoris, vastus medialis/lateralis/intermedius | 160 deg |
  | Prone ankle dorsiflexion | soleus, gastrocnemius | 15-25 deg |
  (His values: SLR 70/70; knee-chest R100/L115; hip ext 25/25; IR 15/15; ER R60/L40; knee flex R150/L135; DF R20/L25.)

### Lecture 2: Acceleration speed (p.48-58)
- Outcomes: key determinants of acceleration; technical model; coaching model; EPI acceleration program design (p.48).
- Sprinting = "a movement skill involving switching on and off of almost every muscle in the body" (p.49). (Slide of 100 m records
  and animal speeds: Bolt ~29 mph.)
- ACCELERATION GOAL (p.50): "Generate as much horizontal force as possible in the least amount of time while maximizing technique".
- Acceleration METRICS (p.50): forward lean 45 deg body angle; first-step stride length 1.5 m; foot height at 1st step 15-30 cm;
  velocity at 10 m >8 m/s (29 km/h); ground contact time 0.12-0.20 s.
- Determinants, SIMPLIFIED (p.50): TECHNIQUE (limbs: arms, legs, posture); FORCE PRODUCTION (explosive force from stationary);
  DIRECTION OF FORCE (horizontal); CONTACT TIME (short GCT <0.15 s).
- Determinants IN-DEPTH (p.51): mechanical factor -> physical determinant: triple extension -> leg extension strength; 45 deg lean ->
  trunk stiffness & control; ankle dorsiflexed -> leg stiffness (ankle); short GCT -> SSC / RFD (and leg stiffness); explosive piston
  leg drive -> speed-strength; explosive arm drive -> upper-body power.
- GPS elite football (Toulouse FC, Rave et al. 2020) per position (p.51): total distance m / HSR m / SPR m / Acc n / Dec n / max speed km/h:
  centre back 9962/493/217/29/22/34; midfielder 12045/928/353/39/62/34; wide midfielder 10340/756/378/47/55/34; attackers 10415/643/348/59/59/35.
  Acc = accelerating efforts up to 3 m/s2; Dec = decelerating efforts up to -3 m/s2.
- TECHNICAL MODEL (p.52): maintain elbow bend; lead hand past eye level; triple extension ankle-knee-hip; shin angle parallel to torso;
  ankle dorsiflexed; foot 15-30 cm off the ground. BACKSIDE mechanics: triple extension; 45-60 deg forward lean. FRONTSIDE: arm drive past
  eye level; shin & torso parallel; no heel drop on ground contact. Error example: excessive forward lean; foot "lands" instead of
  striking backwards.
- COACHING MODEL key areas (p.53): 1 sprint technique (wall drills); 2 acceleration sprints; 3 develop FV curve (get stronger!);
  4 improve contact time (frequent sprint exposure; plyometric training); 5 improve mobility.
- Key messages (p.54, citing a systematic review "Sprinting performance and resistance-based training interventions"): strength training
  incl. general strength, dynamic and unilateral exercises increases speed; NO magic exercises, the magic is putting all pieces together.
- SESSION DESIGN (p.54): 1 dynamic warm-up (RAMP); 2 speed technique (acceleration wall drills); 3 high-intensity sprints (5-30 m);
  4 overload training (resisted sprinting, hill sprints, etc.).
- SPEED RULE #1: do not sprint when tired, sore or mentally stressed. RULE #2: do not perform sprints at the END of a session (p.55).
- PROGRAM DESIGN VARIABLES, acceleration (p.56): intensity 95-100 %; distances 5-30 m; rest 60 s per 10 m; session volume 150-250 m;
  methods flat, uphill, resisted sprinting (sleds, Flexvit bands); key cue "Push the ground away"; strength focus: develop FV curve,
  prioritize plyometrics and hamstring strength. "Keep the goal the goal".
- SAMPLE acceleration session (p.56): RAMP; wall march progression; sprints 1x(5x10 m) 30-60 s rest (50 m) + 1x(5x30 m) 90 s-3 min rest
  (150 m), 5 min between sets; resisted sled sprints 1x(3x15 m) @ 10 % bodyweight (45 m).
- Final thoughts (p.57): do the basics well, don't overcomplicate; #1 test and re-test speed; #2 sprinting at <90 % of max speed does not
  improve speed (Haugen et al., "Not quite so fast": training at 90 % sprint speed) -> "not sprinting is not sprinting".
- Summary (p.58): know your technical model to spot errors; implement coaching model consistently over time; keep the goal the goal.

### Section 3: RAMP warm-up (p.59-60)
- RAMP = Raise, Activate, Mobilize, Potentiate: 4-phase prep so the neuromuscular system is optimally prepared (p.59).
- Sample (p.60): RAISE 3-4 min, general movement skills over 20 m and back (linear running, lateral shuffles, carioca, carioca with high
  knee, low skip to high skip); can use ladders; rule: linear session -> linear raise drills; agility session -> add multi-directional.
  ACTIVATE 2-3 min, glutes/core/VMO/hamstrings: single-leg hip lifts 1x5-6 each; side plank lifts with leg abduction 1x5-6 each side;
  single-leg quarter squat 1x5-6 each; single-leg T-stance 1x5-6 each. MOBILIZE 5-6 min dynamic stretching of lower extremity, lower back,
  hips. POTENTIATE 3-4 min sub-maximal sprints 3x20 m at 70/80/90 %.

### Section 4: Acceleration speed practical (p.61-)
- Apply the technical model, improve technique via the coaching model, deliver a speed session (p.61).
- TECHNICAL MODEL table (p.62), 7 points: 1 FOOT height 15-30 cm off ground; 2 ANKLE dorsiflexed (toe pointing forward); 3 SHIN parallel
  with torso (positive shin angle); 4 KNEE drive (knee pointing forward); 5 HAND to eye level; 6 BODY ANGLE 45-60 deg; 7 TRIPLE EXTENSION
  from push leg.
- SPEED MECHANICS PROFILING (p.63-64): FRONTSIDE markers (in front of body): 01 hand: lead hand past eye level; 02 shin: shin and torso
  parallel; 03 heel: heel stays off ground during contact. BACKSIDE marker: 01 leg: does the push-off leg triple extend?
- SPEED MECHANICS DRILLS, wall series (p.65): SET-UP: body lean 45-60 deg; power line ankle-to-shoulders; weight on ball of foot; abs
  tight; neutral spine; arms parallel with floor. MARCHES: pull left leg through low to high; foot finishes 15-30 cm from floor; ankle
  dorsiflexed; shin parallel with torso; knee pointing forward; power line maintained. SWITCHES: start left foot forward 15-30 cm off ground;
  in one fast dynamic motion switch limbs simultaneously so left becomes support and right foot finishes 15-30 cm off ground.

### Lecture 3: Olympic weightlifting (p.66-75)
- Outcomes: basics before coaching OL; technical model C&J; coaching model C&J; integrating lifts into program design (p.66).
- PHASES (p.67): CLEAN: 1 start position, 2 first pull, 3 transition, 4 second pull, 5 catch, 6 recovery. JERK: 1 dip, 2 drive, 3 catch,
  4 recovery (p.67-68, photo sequences; videos of C&J and clean).
- Teaching C&J FUNDAMENTALS (p.69): 1 technical model; 2 coaching model; 3 clarity in program design; 4 demonstration skills; 5 coaching eye.
- CLEAN TECHNICAL MODEL:
  - START POSITION (p.69), 11 points: feet shoulder width; bar over 1st MTP joint of big toe; pronated HOOK grip; knees in front of bar;
    hips higher than knees; shoulders in front of bar; back flat; scapulae retracted; elbows straight, pointing outwards; neck slight
    extension; weight mid- to forefoot.
  - FIRST PULL (p.70): bar pulled slightly in towards body from floor; hips & shoulders rise at SAME rate; posterior knee drive; back and
    arms straight; scapulae retracted; arms straight; first pull finishes just above knees; weight shifts midfoot -> rear foot.
  - SECOND PULL (p.70): torso to upright; bar contacts UPPER THIGH; triple extension ankle-knee-hip; heels lift; aggressive shrug;
    elbows bend slightly; bar close to body; weight starts midfoot, ends forefoot.
  - CATCH (p.70): at highest point of 2nd pull start flexing elbows; loosen grip, rotate elbows quickly under bar; transfer weight down;
    land in FRONT SQUAT position, bar on anterior deltoids; land with wider stance for stability; weight shifts to rear foot.
  (Transition and recovery phases are listed but have no separate bullet slide in this manual.)
- COACHING MODEL (p.71): establish flexibility & mobility first: shoulder, T-spine, hip, ankle. TEACH FRONT SQUAT FIRST before introducing
  OL. Clean drills: PHASE 1 "crease of hip" position: shrug & triple extend, front squat, clean land, clean drop, receive the bar, jump & catch.
  PHASE 2 hang: hang slides, hang shrug & triple extend, hang clean. PHASE 3 floor: clean pull, power clean, squat clean.
- PROGRAM DESIGN (p.72-74): developing the clean = 3 areas: technique, strength, speed.
  - STRENGTH development, max strength (85 %+ 1RM) main exercises: back squat, front squat, clean deadlift.
  - STRENGTH-SPEED (80 %+ 1RM): squat clean (80-100 %), clean pull (100-120 %).
  - SPEED development, SPEED-STRENGTH (60-80 % 1RM): hang cleans (60-70 %), power cleans (70-80 %), high pulls (60-80 %), *loaded plyometrics.
  - SPEED development, REACTIVE STRENGTH (0-0.5 s GCT): box jumps (0.25-0.5 s), broad jumps (0.25-0.5 s), depth jumps (0-0.25 s).
  (FV-curve diagrams place these from high force to high velocity: max strength -> strength-speed -> speed-strength -> reactive strength.)
  - Variables (p.74): sets 3-4; reps 1-4 (5-6 with empty bar when learning); rest 2-3 min novice-intermediate, 3-4 min intermediate-advanced;
    intensity strength-speed 80 %+, speed-strength 60-80 %; frequency typically 1-2 OL sessions/week.
  - SAMPLE lower-body STRENGTH-SPEED session (p.74): squat clean 3x2 @90 % 3 min; trap-bar jumps 3x4 @50 % 3 min; BB step-ups 3x10 90 s;
    SL DB RDL 3x10 60 s; SL calf raises 3x10 60 s; core: plate V-ups 3x15; seated Russian twists 3x10 ES.
  - SAMPLE lower-body SPEED-STRENGTH session (p.75): hang clean 3x4 @70 % 2-3 min; trap-bar jumps 3x4 @30 % 2-3 min; DB Bulgarian squats 3x10
    90 s; SL Swiss-ball hamstring rolls 3x10 60 s; SL calf switches 3x10 60 s; core: hanging toes-to-bar 3x10; SB Russian twists 3x10 ES.
  - Summary: technical model to spot errors; coaching model consistently; keep the goal the goal.

### Section 5: Clean & Jerk practical (p.76-78)
- EPI "three phase top-down approach" (p.76).
- Key points (p.77): always start novices with Phase 1 drills before Phase 2 and 3; use the BAR ONLY; system based on learning with empty bar;
  3-4 sessions/training units to teach and train each phase.
- Clean set-up: hook grip; grip 2-3 inches outside hip width; feet hip/shoulder width pointing straight; good posture; "sit position"
  (slight flexion ankle, knee, hip).
- Drill tables with START POSITION (p.77-78): PHASE 1 sit position: 1 shrug & triple extend (sit position); 2 front squat (rack position);
  3 clean land (rack); 4 clean drop, full squat (rack); 5 receiving the bar high-high & low-low (rib cage & belly button); 6 jump & catch
  (sit position). PHASE 2 hang: hang slides, hang shrug & triple extend, hang clean (all hang position). PHASE 3 floor: clean pull, power clean,
  squat clean (floor). NOTE: the lecture calls Phase 1 "crease of hip position", the practical calls it "sit position"; same drills.
- NOTE: the Jerk has phases listed but no written technical/coaching model in Phase 1 manual pages seen.

### Lecture 4: Program design (p.80-)
- Outcomes: key program variables; exercise selection for specific strength qualities; write strength & power sessions using EPI's system.
- 8 KEY VARIABLES (p.81): 1 sports needs analysis (primary vs secondary needs: movement, strength, speed/ESD, injuries); 2 training age
  (and movement ability); 3 periodization model & period (e.g. pre-season / in-season); 4 program goal (which physical quality);
  5 training frequency (sessions per week); 6 session design (training methods, exercise selection, exercise order); 7 intensity & volume
  (% 1RM; dosage: maximum vs minimum effective dose); 8 rest periods (appropriate for each strength quality).
- REP MAX CONTINUUM (p.82): reps <=2 ... >=20 vs training goal: strength emphasised at low reps (~<=6), power (*) at low reps, hypertrophy
  ~6-12, muscular endurance ~12-20+, with overlap (colour gradient; exact boundaries graphic-only).
- PHYSICAL QUALITY PROGRAM VARIABLES (p.82) [key table]:
  | Session goal | Exercises | Sets | Reps | Intensity | Rest | Type |
  | Strength endurance | 6-8 | 2-3 | 15+ | 50-70 % | 45-60 s | EXTENSIVE |
  | Hypertrophy | 5-6 | 3-4 | 6-12 | 70-85 % | 60 s-2 min | EXTENSIVE |
  | Max strength | 4-5 | 3-5 | 1-5 | 85 %+ | 3 min | INTENSIVE |
  | Explosive strength | 4-5 | 3-5 | 1-4 | 30-100 % | 3 min | INTENSIVE |
  | Reactive strength | 4-5 | 3-5 | 4-5 | 0-30 % | 2-3 min | INTENSIVE |
- KEY POINT ON DOSAGE (p.82): for each FV-curve strength quality choose only TWO exercises that develop that quality; remaining exercises =
  assistance strength.
- SESSION DESIGN STRUCTURE (p.83-85): warm-up always 1 mobility, 2 activation, 3 movement preparation; extras = core training / prehab.
  LOWER BODY: power -> bilateral strength -> assistance (unilateral strength, posterior chain). UPPER BODY: power -> bilateral strength ->
  assistance (vertical push/pull, horizontal push/pull, accessory). FULL BODY: LB power, LB bilateral, LB assistance, UB power, UB bilateral,
  UB assistance. Linear-periodization session types (LB): HYPERTROPHY = bilateral + assistance (no power); STRENGTH = bilateral + assistance;
  POWER = power + assistance.
- EXERCISE CLASSIFICATION (p.86-88): LB POWER: power clean, hang snatch; hex-bar jumps, BB squat jumps; prowler/sled sprint; speed squat,
  speed deadlift; box jump, depth jump. LB BILATERAL: back squat, deadlift, front squat, RDL, hip thrust. LB UNILATERAL: lunge, step-up, split

- MECHANICAL FACTORS (p.49): body angle UPRIGHT; stride length 2.25-2.7 m; velocity 12.5 m/s (45 km/h); GCT 0.08-0.10 s; 4-5 contacts per s.
- DETERMINANTS (p.49): technique; FORCE PRODUCTION under high ECCENTRIC demands; DIRECTION of force VERTICAL; contact time very short <0.10 s.
- ACCELERATION vs MAX VELOCITY (p.49): steps 1-3 GCT 0.2 s, force horizontal -> 60 m GCT 0.08 s, force vertical. As speed increases:
  GCT down; concentric demand down; eccentric demand up; stiffness requirement up.
- TECHNICAL MODEL slide (p.50): elbow bent; upright torso; hand to eye level; HIGH THIGH position; positive shin angle; ankle dorsiflexed.
  BACKSIDE: active heel recovery. FRONTSIDE: arm drive past eye level; thigh parallel with ground; no heel drop on contact.
- COACHING MODEL key areas (p.51): 1 sprint technique (wall drills, A series, dribble series); 2 max velocity sprints; 3 develop FV curve
  (ECCENTRIC & ELASTIC focus); 4 muscle focus: HAMSTRINGS & GLUTES; 5 improve contact time (frequent sprint exposure, plyometrics).
- SESSION DESIGN (p.52): RAMP warm-up; speed technique (max-velocity wall drills, A series); high-intensity sprints max velocity 30-60 m;
  "dosage very important... less is more!".
- SPEED RULE #1: max velocity sprinting is neurologically very demanding. #2: sprinting is a skill, needs rhythm and feel to stay relaxed.
- PROGRAM VARIABLES max velocity (p.53): intensity 95-100 %; distances 30-60 m; rest 60 s per 10 m; session volume 300-500 m; methods flat,
  ASSISTED and DOWNHILL; strength focus FV curve, eccentric strength and fast SSC. (Compare acceleration: 5-30 m, 150-250 m, resisted/uphill.)
- SPEED FRAMEWORK (general -> specific), yearly plan (p.54): STRENGTH (functional hypertrophy, max strength, max isometric strength) -> POWER

### Lecture 5: Energy system development (ESD) (p.64-83)
- Outcomes: fundamentals; traditional approach; advanced methods.
- Comparison (p.65): ATP-CP = Noah Lyles 100 m (Ferrari); anaerobic glycolysis = Wayde van Niekerk 400 m (BMW); aerobic = Jakob Ingebrigtsen
  3,000-5,000 m (Toyota).
- ATP = energy currency (p.66): every movement needs contraction; contraction needs energy; currency is ATP; when exhausted the body uses other
  sources to RESYNTHESISE ATP; method depends on volume and intensity.
- VI curve (p.66): extensive = aerobic, anaerobic glycolysis; intensive (higher intensity, lower volume) = ATP-CP -> acceleration speed -> max
  velocity speed.
- SIMPLE STRATEGY (traditional):
  - ATP-CP (INTENSIVE) (p.67): training stress neurological (high) vs metabolic (low); classification PCr POWER (6-8 s) & PCr CAPACITY (10-15 s);
    intensity 90-95 % (power) / 85-90 % (capacity); method RST; themes linear & multi-directional; work:rest 1:5-1:10 (power) / 1:3-1:6
    (capacity); fitness test PHOSPHATE DECREMENT TEST (PDT). [NOTE: Phase 1 p.99 gives capacity 9-15 s, 85-95 %, rest 4-6:1, power 95 %, rest
    5-10:1: slight differences between manuals.]
  - Weeks 1-4 progressive overload ATP-CP: goal hold speed over longer distances with quicker recoveries (e.g. 6 s -> 8 s @95 %); weeks 1&2 same
    session, increase target distance; weeks 3&4 increase work duration, same session, increase distance.
  - PCr POWER sessions: wk1 6 s sprint target 40 m, 3x6 @90-95 %, rest 30 s / 5 min; wk2 45 m, 3x6 @95 %; wk3 8 s sprint 55 m 3x4 @90-95 %,
    40 s / 5 min; wk4 60 m 3x4 @95 % (p.67-68).
  - ANAEROBIC GLYCOLYSIS (EXTENSIVE) (p.68): stress neurological (moderate) vs metabolic (high); classification LACTIC POWER (15-30 s) & LACTIC
    CAPACITY (30-90 s); intensity 90 %+ HRmax / 8+/10 Borg RPE; methods intensive intervals & SSG; themes linear & multi-directional; work:rest
    1:1-1:3 (linear vs multi-directional); fitness test 150 m SHUTTLE TEST. Overload goal: work longer distances/time (e.g. 30 s -> 45 s @80 %)
    whilst buffering lactate.
  - ANAEROBIC POWER sessions (p.69): wk1 30 s sprint target 190 m 2x4 @85 % rest 90 s / 5 min (volume 1,520 m); wk2 200 m (1,600 m);
    wk3 45 s sprint 280 m 2x3 @80 % 2 min / 6 min (1,680 m); wk4 290 m (1,740 m).
  - AEROBIC (EXTENSIVE) (p.69): stress neurological low vs metabolic high; classification AEROBIC POWER (90 s-3 min) & AEROBIC CAPACITY (3 min+);
    intensity HR 70-80 % (power) / 60-70 % (capacity); methods extensive intervals & SSG; linear & multi-directional; work:rest 1:0.5-1:1.
    Overload goal: longer distances/time (e.g. 90 s -> 3 min) using oxygen more efficiently.
  - AEROBIC POWER sessions (p.70): wk1 90 s runs 400 m 2x3 @80 % MHR rest 90 s / 5 min (2,400 m); wk2 2x4 (3,200 m); wk3 3 min runs 700 m 2x3
    @70 % MHR 2 min / 6 min (4,200 m); wk4 2x4 (5,600 m).
- ADVANCED STRATEGY (p.71-): PHYSICAL PREPARATION PUZZLE 5 pieces: 1 HIIT fundamentals; 2 key markers & profiling; 3 training methods;
  4 program goals; 5 planning & periodization.
- HIIT definition (p.72): repeated bouts of high-intensity work ABOVE the lactate threshold interspersed with low-intensity exercise or rest.
  4 sub-categories: LONG (extensive) intervals; SHORT (intensive) intervals; REPEATED SPRINT TRAINING (RST); SMALL SIDED GAMES (SSG).
  Diagrams (p.72-73): long intervals ~at/above MLSS-VO2max for long bouts; short intervals briefer bouts above VO2max line; RST near max speed
  very short bouts; SSG variable intensities.
- VI CURVE / ASR diagram (p.73): MSS at top; 180 % vVO2max = 95 % MSS (ST = sprint training); 160 % vVO2max = 85 % MSS (RST zone 85-95 % MSS);
  140 % vVO2max = 75 % MSS; 120 % vVO2max; MAS SHORT intervals ~vVO2max-75 % MSS (100-140 %? per bracket); vVO2max = MAS; 90 % vVO2max = MLSS;
  MAS LONG intervals around MLSS-MAS. ASR spans MAS -> MSS. Abbreviations: ASR anaerobic speed reserve; MSS maximal sprint speed; ST sprint
  training; RST repeated sprint training; MAS maximal aerobic speed; MLSS maximal lactate steady state.
- MLSS (p.74): highest intensity sustainable without continuous lactate build-up (aka anaerobic threshold); as % VO2max: untrained 70-80 %,
  trained 80-90 %.
- KEY MARKER 1: MAS (p.74) = lowest speed at which VO2max occurs (= vVO2max); m/s or km/h; assessed with BRONCO (multi-directional) or 1.5 km
  time trial (linear). CALCULATION: Bronco = 5 x 240 m = 1,200 m (shuttles to 20, 40, 60 m lines and back); score 4:25 = 265 s;
  MAS = 1200/265 = 4.5 m/s; x3.6 = 16.2 km/h = vVO2max.
- CLASSIFY TEAM MAS (p.75): good <4:35 -> av 4.5 m/s (16.2 km/h); average 4:35-4:50 -> 4.25 m/s (15.3 km/h); poor >4:50 -> 4 m/s (14.4 km/h).
  [NOTE: Phase 1 p.21 profile bands differ: good <4:30, avg 4:30-4:45, poor >4:45.]
- KEY MARKER 2: MSS (p.75) = maximal speed over 30-60 m; assessed with 10-30 m flying sprint (diagram: 30 m run-in + 20 m timed).
  Example: 20 m in 2.1 s -> 9.5 m/s -> 34.2 km/h. TEAM MSS (p.76): good <2.1 s (9.5 m/s, 35 km/h); average 2.1-2.3 (9.1 m/s, 33.5 km/h);
  poor >2.3 (8.7 m/s, 31.2 km/h).
- KEY MARKER 3: ASR (p.76) = capacity difference between vVO2max/MAS and MSS. ASR = MSS (km/h) - MAS (km/h).
  Example: A MAS 16 km/h (4.45 m/s), MSS 30 km/h (8.3 m/s), ASR 14 km/h (3.8 m/s); B MAS 16, MSS 34 km/h (9.4 m/s), ASR 18 km/h (5 m/s).
  HIIT short interval Eurofit 15:15 at 120 % MAS = 19 km/h for both -> 120 % MAS = 21.5 % of A's ASR vs 16.5 % of B's (p.77 graph): same %MAS
  is relatively harder for A.
  PRACTICAL SOLUTION (p.77): prescribe as % of ASR: 15 s @ 100 % MAS + 20 % ASR -> A runs 18.8 km/h, B 19.6 km/h.
- TRAINING METHODS (p.78-79):
  - Method 2 SHORT INTERVAL (GROUPING) (extensive): Eurofit 1:1 (15:15 for 5-10 min) @110-120 % MAS; MAS 4.45 m/s; session 3 x 5 min, 3 min rest;
    distance = MAS (m/s) x intensity x work (s); e.g. 4.45 x 1.2 x 15 = 80 m. Eurofit set-up groups at 120 %: good 81 m; average 77 m; poor 72 m.
  - Method 2 SHORT INTERVAL (INDIVIDUALIZED): 15:15 for 5-10 min @100 % MAS + 15-20 % ASR: A 4.5 + 0.75 = 5.25 m/s; B 4.5 + 1 = 5.5 m/s;
    3 x 5 min, 3 min rest; distance A 5.25 x 1 x 15 = 78 m; B 5.5 x 1 x 15 = 82 m.
    [Method 1 (long intervals) is not given its own card here; it appears as Phase 1 of the program goals below.]
  - Method 3 RST (INTENSIVE): PCr power 6-8 s @90-95 % MSS; A MSS 30 km/h = 8.3 m/s (95 % = 7.6 m/s); B 34 km/h = 9.4 m/s (95 % = 8.9 m/s).
    Session 3 x 5 for 6 s @95 %, work:rest 1:5 (30 s). Distance = 95 % speed x 6 s minus 5 m for acceleration: A 45 - 5 = 40 m; B 53 - 5 = 48 m.
- PROGRAM GOALS (p.80) progressive overload of HIIT, 3 phases (each weeks 1-4):

  - PHASE 3 RST (develop intensity), PCr power: wk1 3x4 for 6 s @95 %; wk2 3x6 for 6 s; wk3 3x4 for 8 s; wk4 3x6 for 8 s (all @95 %).
- 4 steps to planning (same as Phase 1). CONJUGATE MODEL THEMES (p.81): annual plan: preparatory (GPP, SPP) -> competition (pre-comp, comp)
  -> transition; CONJUGATE runs EXTENSIVE 1/2/3 AND INTENSIVE 1/2/3 in parallel across GPP, SPP, pre-comp (both themes every block).

### Section 5: Plyometric training, FAST SSC (p.97-101)
- PHASE 1 fundamentals (coordination for fast hopping/jumping; session 1): SL pogos (supported): fully supported -> fully supported & toe up ->
  half supported & toe up. Pogos (5 m): forward & toe up -> lateral (L&R) & toe up -> backward & toe up.

  jump distance guys 3-4 jumps, girls 4-5 [i.e. number of jumps to cover 10 m].

- RSI TESTING (p.101): 2-3 depth jumps at a range of heights (e.g. 15, 30, 45, 60 cm), record jump height, contact time and RSI at each =
  fast SSC profile. If RSI maintained or improves with height AND GCT stays <0.25 s -> capacity sufficient for that drop height. If RSI drops or
  GCT >0.25 s -> sub-optimal stimulus or excessive muscle-tendon stress.
  RSI bands: <1.5 low (unprepared for moderate plyos; focus max strength + slow SSC); 1.5-2.0 moderate (prepared for moderate intensity; begin

  >3.0 world class (maintain elastic strength bi- & unilaterally).
